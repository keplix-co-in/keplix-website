If your car breaks down on a highway, your first job is not the car. It is getting everyone safely away from moving traffic. Switch on the hazard lights, steer as far left as you can, get all passengers out on the side away from traffic and behind the crash barrier if there is one, and only then call for help. On national highways, NHAI's 24x7 helpline is 1033. For any accident or injury, call 112, India's single emergency number.

Highway traffic moves fast, and drivers do not expect a stationary car in a lane. A stopped car is at far greater risk than a moving one, and so are the people standing next to it. Everything below follows from that.

1. As the car starts to fail

  • Switch on the hazard lights immediately, the moment you sense a problem, not once you have stopped.
  • Move left steadily. Indicate, check your mirrors and move towards the left shoulder while you still have momentum. Do not brake hard in a fast lane.
  • Aim for the safest spot you can reach: a wide shoulder, a lay-by, a service road, or past a bend rather than on it, so oncoming drivers can see you from a distance. Avoid stopping on a bridge, in a tunnel or just over the crest of a hill if the car can make it a little further.
  • If the car stops in a lane and will not move, keep the hazards on and treat getting people out safely as the priority.

2. Once you have stopped

  1. Hazards on, parking brake on, wheels turned away from the road. If the car is hit from behind, wheels turned to the left help keep it from rolling into traffic.
  2. Get everyone out on the left side, away from traffic. Do not open the right-hand doors onto a live lane.
  3. Move everyone behind the crash barrier, or as far from the carriageway as the ground allows, and stand ahead of or level with the car, not behind it. A car that is hit can be pushed forward.
  4. Do not wait inside the car on a high-speed road unless there is no safe place to stand. Being inside does not protect you from a rear-end impact.
  5. At night, wear something light or reflective if you have it, and use the phone torch so you are seen.
  6. Keep children and pets under control at all times. A dog or a child running onto the road is the biggest risk once you are out.

3. Place the warning triangle

A reflective warning triangle gives drivers behind you advance notice that the road ahead is blocked. Place it well behind the car, on the same side of the road, so that drivers see it with enough time to change lanes. On a fast highway, that means considerably further back than people expect; on a bend or a slope, place it before the bend so it is visible to traffic coming round it.

Only place the triangle if you can do it without walking into traffic. Walk along the shoulder or behind the barrier, facing oncoming vehicles, and carry the triangle in front of you so it is visible. If the shoulder is narrow or traffic is heavy, skip it: your safety matters more than the triangle.

4. Call for help

NHAI helpline: 1033

1033 is the National Highways Authority of India's 24x7 toll-free helpline for national highways. It can be used to report a breakdown, an accident or a medical emergency, and the number is printed on signboards along highways and on FASTags. Some reports say assistance is focused on tolled stretches, so on a non-tolled national highway or a state highway, also try your other options below.

Emergency: 112

If anyone is injured, the car is on fire, or you feel unsafe, call 112. It is India's Emergency Response Support System number, and connects to police, fire and ambulance services.

Your own roadside assistance

Many car makers offer roadside assistance, especially on newer cars, and many motor insurance policies include it as an add-on. Check your insurance papers and the owner's manual now, and save the numbers in your phone, so you are not searching for them on a dark shoulder.

5. What to tell the tow or assistance provider

A clear first call saves a lot of time. Have this ready:

  • Exact location: highway number, direction of travel (for example, "towards Jaipur"), the nearest kilometre stone or toll plaza, and any landmark such as a dhaba, petrol pump or flyover. Share a live location pin on WhatsApp or by SMS if they accept it.
  • Which side of the road you are on, especially on a divided highway, where reaching the other carriageway can mean a long detour.
  • Car make, model, colour and registration number.
  • Fuel type, including CNG, and whether it is manual or automatic. Many automatics and four-wheel-drive cars should not be towed with the driven wheels on the ground; a flatbed may be needed. Check your owner's manual.
  • What happened: the symptom, any warning lights, smoke, noises or smells. "Overheated and lost power" or "flat tyre, spare also flat" lets them bring the right help.
  • How many people are with you, and whether anyone needs to travel with the tow.

Before the car is towed, ask where it is being taken, what the charge will be, and get the driver's name and phone number. Take photos of the car from all sides before it is loaded.

Things you should not do

  • Do not try to repair anything on the traffic side of the car.
  • Do not open the radiator cap on an overheated engine. Hot coolant under pressure can cause serious burns.
  • Do not accept a tow from an unknown passer-by without agreeing the destination and the charge first.
  • Do not walk along the carriageway to find help if you can call instead.

What to keep in the car

Most of this is cheap and takes up little space:

  • A reflective warning triangle.
  • A working spare tyre at the correct pressure, plus the jack and wheel spanner. Check the spare every time you check the other tyres; our tyre care guide covers pressures.
  • A torch with fresh batteries, or a rechargeable one.
  • A reflective vest or jacket.
  • Jump leads.
  • A phone charging cable and a power bank.
  • A first-aid kit.
  • Drinking water, especially in summer.
  • Your RC, insurance and PUC certificate, either physical copies or in DigiLocker.
  • A written list of emergency numbers: 1033, 112, your roadside assistance number and your insurer's claims line.

Prevent the most common breakdowns

Many highway breakdowns come from a handful of causes that a quick pre-trip check would catch: low tyre pressure, a weak battery, low coolant and overdue servicing. Before a long drive, check tyres including the spare, and if the car is due, get a pre-trip service done. If you do break down, you can request breakdown assistance on Keplix.
