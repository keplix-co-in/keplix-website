"Alignment and balancing" are said so often in one breath that many drivers assume they are the same job. They are not. They fix different problems, use different machines, and are needed at different times. Knowing which is which helps you describe the problem accurately and avoid paying for work the car does not need.

Wheel balancing: about weight

No tyre and wheel are perfectly uniform. Some spots are slightly heavier than others. At highway speed, that small heavy spot is spinning many times a second, and the imbalance turns into a vibration you can feel.

Balancing puts the wheel on a machine that spins it, finds the heavy spot, and tells the technician where to clip or stick small weights on the rim to cancel it out.

Signs you need balancing

  • The steering wheel vibrates or shimmies at a particular speed range, often somewhere between 60 and 100 km/h, and smooths out above or below it.
  • A vibration felt through the seat or floor rather than the steering wheel often means the rear wheels are out of balance.
  • You can see a gap where a balancing weight used to be on the rim.

When balancing is needed

  • Every time a tyre is fitted to a rim: new tyres, or a puncture repair that needed the tyre taken off the rim.
  • When a weight has fallen off.
  • When a new vibration appears after hitting a pothole hard.

Wheel alignment: about angles

Alignment is about the angles at which the wheels sit relative to the car and to each other. The main ones are:

  • Toe: whether the fronts of the wheels point slightly inward or outward, seen from above.
  • Camber: whether the tops of the wheels tilt in or out, seen from the front.
  • Caster: the angle of the steering axis, which affects how the steering self-centres.

Potholes, speed breakers taken too fast, kerb strikes and worn suspension parts all knock these angles out of specification. Alignment uses a machine with sensors or cameras mounted on the wheels to measure the angles and adjust them back to the manufacturer's figures.

Not every angle can be adjusted on every car. On many hatchbacks and compact sedans, front toe is the only adjustment the design allows. Camber and caster are fixed and only change if something is bent or worn. If the bill lists "camber correction" on a car like that, ask what exactly was adjusted and how.

Signs you need alignment

  • The car drifts or pulls to one side on a straight, flat road.
  • The steering wheel sits off-centre when you are driving straight.
  • Tyres are wearing unevenly, for example faster on the inner or outer edge. This is the costliest symptom, because it quietly destroys tyres.
  • Tyres squeal on gentle turns at low speed.

When alignment is needed

  • After fitting new tyres, to protect the investment.
  • After replacing steering or suspension parts such as tie rods or lower arms.
  • After a hard pothole or kerb impact, if the car now drives differently.
  • As a periodic check. Many garages in India suggest a check every 5,000–10,000 km on rough roads. Follow your manual where it gives a figure.

Quick reference

  • Vibration at a certain speed → usually balancing.
  • Pulling to one side, or an off-centre steering wheel → usually alignment.
  • Uneven tyre wear → alignment, and possibly worn suspension parts.

When it is neither

Some symptoms look like alignment or balance problems but are not:

  • Pulling only when braking usually points to the brakes, such as a seized caliper piston or contaminated pads, or to worn suspension bushes, not to alignment. Our post on brake warning signs covers this in more detail.
  • A pull that changes after swapping the front tyres left to right can be a tyre fault rather than alignment.
  • Vibration that appears only under braking is often warped or unevenly worn brake discs.
  • Clunks over bumps point to worn suspension parts. Aligning a car with worn parts will not hold, so those parts need replacing first.

What to ask for

A good garage prints or shows you the alignment readings before and after adjustment, with the manufacturer's specification range alongside. Ask for that printout. It is the only way to know the work was actually done, and it is useful to compare next time.
